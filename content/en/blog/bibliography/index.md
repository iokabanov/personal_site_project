## What is a Bibliography?

A bibliography is a structured list of sources used in a research paper, essay, 
or any academic work. It gives credit to the original authors, allows readers to 
verify your sources, and demonstrates the depth of your research. In academia, 
proper bibliography management is considered an essential skill.

## Why Does It Matter?

Poorly formatted or incomplete references can seriously undermine an otherwise 
strong piece of academic writing. Beyond just formatting, keeping track of your 
sources as you research saves a huge amount of time later when writing up your work.

## Citation Styles

There are several major citation styles used across different fields:

- **APA** (American Psychological Association): widely used in social sciences and psychology.
- **MLA** (Modern Language Association): common in humanities and literature.
- **IEEE**: standard in engineering and computer science.
- **Chicago**: used in history and some social sciences.

Each style has its own rules for how to format books, journal articles, websites, 
and other source types. Always check which style your institution or journal requires.

## Tools for Managing Bibliography

Doing bibliography manually is tedious and error-prone. Thankfully there are great 
tools that automate the process:

**Zotero** is a free, open-source reference manager that automatically collects 
bibliographic information from websites and academic databases. It integrates with 
Word, LibreOffice, and Google Docs.

**Mendeley** is another popular reference manager, particularly common in STEM fields. 
It also doubles as an academic social network where you can follow researchers in 
your field.

**BibTeX** is the standard bibliography tool used with LaTeX. You store all your 
references in a `.bib` file and cite them in your document using simple keys like 
`\cite{author2024}`. LaTeX then formats the bibliography automatically according 
to your chosen style.

## Example BibTeX Entry

```bibtex
@article{turing1950,
  author  = {Alan Turing},
  title   = {Computing Machinery and Intelligence},
  journal = {Mind},
  year    = {1950},
  volume  = {59},
  number  = {236},
  pages   = {433--460}
}
```

## Scientific Databases for Finding Sources

When looking for academic sources, these platforms are your best friends:

- **Google Scholar**: the easiest starting point for finding papers on any topic.
- **arXiv**: free preprints in math, physics, computer science, and more.
- **eLibrary**: the main Russian academic database.
- **ResearchGate**: great for finding papers and contacting authors directly.
- **ORCID**: a unique identifier system for researchers, helping you find the 
  right author among many with similar names.

## Tips for Good Bibliography Practice

Start collecting sources from day one of your research, not at the end. Always 
save the full citation information when you find a useful source. Use a reference 
manager rather than doing it by hand. And always double check your final reference 
list against the required citation style before submitting.

Good bibliography habits will save you hours of frustration down the line!
